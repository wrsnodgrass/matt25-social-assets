# FAITHFUL RETIREMENT — Episode Script
## The OBBBA Senior Deduction: A Closing Window to Convert Up to $48,000 Tax-Free
Target runtime: 14–16 min | Teleprompter-ready

---

[HOOK]

If you're married, both over 65, and you don't act before this provision sunsets after 2028, you could miss a window to move up to $48,000 out of your IRA with zero federal tax on it. Today I'm going to show you exactly how this works, and exactly how to tell if it applies to you.

[WHAT CHANGED]

Here's the backdrop. The One Big Beautiful Bill, OBBBA, added a new senior deduction on top of the standard deduction, available for tax years 2025 through 2028. For a married couple filing jointly where both spouses are 65 or older, that new deduction stacks with the existing standard deduction and senior add-on.

Added together, a couple in that situation can push their deduction well past what most people assume they're allowed. For many retirees in the right income range, that opens real room underneath the top of the 0% and 10% tax brackets, room that's sitting unused every year it's not converted.

That room is temporary. The senior deduction phases out at higher incomes and goes away after 2028 unless Congress extends it. This is not a prediction about what Congress will do. It's a fact about what the law says today, and today is what you can plan around.

[QUICK DISCLOSURE — spoken once, brief]

Quick note before I walk through the numbers: the couple I'm about to use is hypothetical, for illustration only. Your bracket, your deduction, and your numbers will be different. Talk to your own advisor before you act on any of this.

[THE WALKTHROUGH]

Let's make this concrete. Picture a married couple, both 65, both retired, pulling income from Social Security and a pension that together land well under the top of the lower brackets. Once you stack the new senior deduction on top of their standard deduction, they have a gap, a dollar range they can pull out of a traditional IRA this year at 0% federal tax, or very close to it.

[GRAPHIC: show the stacked deduction math — standard deduction + senior add-on + OBBBA senior deduction = total shelter]

That gap is the target for a Roth conversion. Instead of letting that bracket space go unused, they convert IRA dollars into a Roth IRA up to the edge of that room. The tax on the conversion is covered, or largely covered, by the deduction stack itself. Money that would eventually come out of that IRA and get taxed at ordinary rates, including in the 22% or 24% bracket once RMDs start, instead moves into a Roth now, where it grows tax-free and comes out tax-free for the rest of their lives, and for their kids.

[GRAPHIC: before/after — IRA balance vs. Roth balance over time, side by side]

This is not free money and it is not a guaranteed return. It is a shift in which bucket future growth sits in, and which tax rate applies to it. For the right household, in the right bracket, in the right years, that shift is worth real dollars over a retirement.

[WHO THIS ACTUALLY FITS]

This strategy is not for everyone. It tends to fit retirees who are in a lower-income year, who have meaningful traditional IRA or 401k balances, who are not yet taking Required Minimum Distributions, and who can pay any conversion tax from money outside the IRA rather than withholding it from the conversion itself. If you're still working full income, or you're already in a high bracket, the math looks different. This is exactly the kind of decision where you run your actual numbers before you act, not someone else's.

[THE DEADLINE]

The senior deduction is scheduled to end after the 2028 tax year. That gives most households four tax years, 2025 through 2028, to use this room if it applies to them. After that, the extra deduction goes away and this particular door closes, regardless of what the rest of the tax code does.

It's a strategy you plan for in January, run the numbers on every year the window is open, and execute on purpose. Good stewardship isn't just giving generously, it's not leaving a government-built door open and walking past it four years in a row.
